Literature

This page provides a comprehensive overview of some of the literature on the education systems of California and Hawaii. Specifically, it centeres around student to teacher ratio, class size, and school quality.

Everything You Need to Know About Student-Teacher Ratios

This article exploes the importance of Student to Teacher ratios. It mentions that better (lower) student to teacher ratios, due to more engagement and less work for teachers, leads to better test scores, fewer dropouts, and higher rates of graduation.

Class Size Matters (California Teachers Association)

This article discusses teacher perspective on the student-teacher ratio debate. Representatives of the California Teachers Association talk about what they think is the ideal ratio and how it affects the quality of the classroom. They argue that full state funding must be allocated to this topic, as it is one of the most important issues in the Calfornia Education System.

What Has Proposition 30 Meant for California?

This article discusses Proposition 30 and 55 and how it affects the quality of education in California. Approved in 2012, Proposition 30 was a policy that allowed for temporary tax rate increases to fight California's daunting fiscal challenges, allowing this money to be put to schools. Specifically, it aimed to tax the wealthy to fund education during this time of need.

A key metric that this proposition affected was student-teacher ratios. As mentioned by the article, the average number of students per teacher has dropped since the approval of Proposition 30 (coupled with Proposition 98) due to the state's newfound ability to reinvest in the school system by significantly increasing spending on education.

Campbell High Overcrowding Hits 'Emergency' Status, School Officials Say

This article discusses overcrowding and high teacher-student ratios in certain Hawaii public schools. Administrators argued that without proper funding, the student to teacher ratio could exceed the already high 27-30:1 ratio for Campbell High School.

Crowded Classrooms, Teacher Shortages – Demographic Changes Could Help Our Nation’s Troubled Schools, but Only if We Let Them

This paper argues that continuous population growth, fueled by high levels of immigration and domestic migration, exacerbates issues of high student-teacher ratios and school popuations, lessening schools' capacities to provide quality education. The author suggests that declining birth rates could naturally alleviate some pressure on the education system, but fears that policies ignoring population increases may counteract this potential relief. He emphasizes the need for a national conversation about the implications of demographic trends on education, advocating for policies that consider the capacity of schools to effectively serve their student populations.
