Welcome!

This website compares the education systems of California and Hawaii, evaluating strengths and weaknesses in each system. This website explores policy, data, previous literature, and more to carefully analyze the differences and similarities of these two states, eventually leading to evidence-based policy recommendations.

State Selection

Growing up in Hawaii, I’ve always envied the educational opportunities of public schools in California. From my private Baptist high school, I watched as the public high schools in California competed in national robotics competitions, participated in debate tournaments, and volunteered with their school’s Key Club. Meanwhile, my school lacked the resources to support these activities, and the Hawaii public schools had even fewer opportunities to offer.

This disparity in resources was one of the driving forces behind my decision to focus on these two states for my analysis. To me, the best private schools on the island were comparable to the average public school in California—public schools had the ability to provide students with more diverse extracurricular programs, more advanced technology, and a wider selection of academic courses. On the other hand, most public schools in Hawaii faced significant challenges related to funding and the availability of academic and extracurricular programs, somethat California just didn’t seem to face.

However, I remember from previous data tasks that both states performed roughly the same on standardized tests. I remember being shocked, as I expected California’s scores to be a lot higher than Hawaii’s due to obvious educational differences. This told me that there was more to be discovered about these two states, and that surface-level comparisons weren’t going to tell the whole story.

This piqued my interest: why does California seem to have greater educational opportunity yet similar test scores? What is Hawaii doing differently to make up for its lack of resources? In evaluating the strengths and weaknesses of these two states, I hope to draw insights about what each school is doing right and how these two educational structures can borrow policies and practices from one another to do better.
